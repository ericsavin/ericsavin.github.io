MG3416-Advanced structural dynamics and acoustics

Graduate course master (MS)2SC (2002-2026)

Vibrations of complex industrial structures at low frequencies are very well represented by their natural eigenmodes (see courses on vibration, modal analysis for example). This representation, however, is no longer at all suitable at high frequencies, for which structures exhibit a radically different behavior. These aspects are of great practical importance, particularly for everything that concerns the internal and/or external acoustics of transport vehicles: automobiles, railways, aircraft, spacecraft, etc. Statistical Energy Analysis (SEA) is the most advanced method for dealing with such problems so far, although it is based on a series of relatively restrictive assumptions. The objectives of this course are twofold: on the one hand the main developments leading to the SEA formulation are presented; on the other hand the hypotheses introduced gradually and which restrict its scope of application, are emphasized.

MG1300-Structural dynamics and acoustics

Undergraduate course (2008-2015 with Didier Clouteau, Régis Cottereau, and Pierre-Étienne Gautier)

Mechanical dynamic phenomena play an essential part in many engineering fields: geophysics, underwater acoustics, bridge resistance to wind and swell, stability and comfort of aeronautical and terrestrial vehicles, rotating machinery, non destructive control, ultrasound scan, actuators... The aim of this course is to provide students with knowledge and essential methods for the analysis and quantification of these phenomena in structural dynamics and acoustics.

  1. Review + Concepts of geometric stiffness. Tutorial: Vibrating string.
  2. Principle of Virtual Work (PVW) – Ritz method – mass and stiffness operators. Tutorial: Application of PVW to rigid bodies.
  3. 3D Finite Elements for dynamic problems – mass and stiffness matrices. Tutorial: Simplified FE model of a dam.
  4. Review of beams/bars – PVW – waves vs. modes. Tutorial: Time-domain approaches.
  5. Natural modes and FE approximation, generalization to general elastic solids, frequency response function: response to point or harmonic excitation. Tutorial: 1-DOF system vibrations + variations (simply supported beam, bridge with moving load).
  6. Stochastic analysis: intro, characterization of loads (time, frequency, random domains) Tutorial: Rice’s formulas; time-domain vs. frequency-domain approaches (Syy = |H|²Sff).
  7. Industrial cases: modal superposition, response spectrum. Integrated computer session.
  8. Acoustics: basic equations, elementary sources (monopole, dipole, etc.). Tutorial: Source identification, acoustic array principle.
  9. Waveguides. Tutorial: Exhaust system, wind instrument.
  10. Fluid-Structure Interaction (light fluid). Tutorial: Dense fluid, added mass.
  11. Industrial application examples: double-wall efficiency – glazing – damping.

1EL5000-Continuum mechanics

Undergraduate course (2002-present)

Continuum mechanics is ubiquitous in any industrial project implying either basic or advanced technology, but also in earth and life sciences. It is at the heart of climate or ice-sheet models, growth of plants and trees, and even the development of an embryo or DNA replication. The basic concepts are introduced in a common unified framework for tridimensional deformable solids and slender structures--beams in particular. Problems involving mechanics at different scales illustrate the course, with some applications to civil engineering, transportation, biomechanics and nanotechnology typically.

Short course on uncertainty quantification for CFD

(2019 with Jacques Peter)

The maturity of Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and the variability of operational and geometrical parameters in fluid dynamics analysis and design lead to the development of Uncertainty Quantification (UQ). Among the numerous methods for UQ, these lecture notes describe the main features of Monte-Carlo and metamodel-based Monte-Carlo, generalized polynomial chaos, and stochastic collocation. The broadly used sparse grid quadrature method of Smolyak is then outlined. Lastly some concepts of variance analysis are introduced. Two applications of UQ to 2D and 3D RANS flows, that have been carried out at ONERA (Computational Fluid Dynamics Dept.), are finally presented.
